<p>Remember when &#8220;software management&#8221; meant updating a few core applications and maybe tracking licenses in a spreadsheet? <em>Ah, simpler times.</em>&nbsp;</p>



<p>Fast forward to today, and your organization is probably using more SaaS applications than you have fingers and toes – each with its own login, its own billing cycle, and its own potential security headache.</p>



<p><a href="/monitor/saas-sprawl-is-a-rising-concern-for-it-causes-challenges-and-best-practices/">The consequences are far-reaching</a>. You&#8217;re looking at increased costs from redundant subscriptions and hidden fees. There are significant security risks due to &#8220;shadow IT&#8221; and unpatched vulnerabilities in unmanaged applications. Compliance issues can arise when you don&#8217;t know where sensitive data resides. Then there&#8217;s the sheer operational inefficiency from data silos, integration headaches, and employees frustrated by context switching between too many tools.</p>



<p>You&#8217;re not alone. The tidal wave of <a href="/what-is-saas-sprawl/">SaaS sprawl</a> is here, and it&#8217;s threatening to drown even the most seasoned IT pro.</p>



<p>Let’s walk you through how to identify, assess, and implement strategies to effectively manage SaaS sprawl, bringing order back to your software ecosystem.</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading">What is SaaS sprawl (and how do you fix it quickly)?</h2>



<p><strong>SaaS sprawl</strong> is the uncontrolled growth of cloud applications across an organization, leading to duplicate tools, wasted spend, and increased security risk.</p>



<p><strong>The fastest way to fix it:</strong></p>



<ol class="wp-block-list">
<li>Discover all SaaS applications in use</li>



<li>Eliminate redundant or unused tools</li>



<li>Right-size licenses based on usage</li>



<li>Enforce centralized procurement and governance</li>
</ol>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-the-symptoms-how-to-identify-saas-sprawl-in-your-organization">The symptoms: How to identify SaaS sprawl in your organization</h2>



<p>Think your organization might be suffering from SaaS sprawl? Here are the tell-tale signs:</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-financial-indicators">Financial indicators</h3>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><a href="/monitor/software-renewals-the-smart-checklist-every-business-needs/"><strong>Unexpected subscription renewals</strong></a><strong>:</strong> Bills arriving for services you barely remember signing up for.</li>



<li><strong>Multiple tools for the same function:</strong> You&#8217;ve got three different project management tools, two video conferencing platforms, and four file-sharing services.</li>



<li><strong>Rising software expenditure without clear ROI:</strong> Your SaaS budget is growing, but you can&#8217;t point to proportional gains in efficiency or productivity.</li>
</ul>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-operational-indicators">Operational indicators</h3>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>Data silos and integration challenges:</strong> Information is trapped in different applications, making a unified view impossible.</li>



<li><strong>Difficulty in tracking software usage:</strong> You have no idea who&#8217;s using what, or how frequently.</li>



<li><strong>Employees using unauthorized software (&#8220;</strong><a href="/monitor/hidden-risks-shadow-it/"><strong>shadow IT</strong></a><strong>&#8220;):</strong> Your team is finding workarounds and using tools outside of your approved list, creating unmanaged access points.</li>



<li><strong>Lack of clear ownership for specific SaaS tools:</strong> No one person or department is responsible for managing a particular application.</li>
</ul>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-security-amp-compliance-indicators">Security &amp; compliance indicators</h3>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>Gaps in security protocols:</strong> Unknown access points from unmonitored SaaS apps create vulnerabilities.</li>



<li><strong>Challenges with data governance and privacy:</strong> You can&#8217;t confidently say where all your sensitive data is stored or who has access to it.</li>



<li><strong>Difficulty in auditing software usage for compliance:</strong> You can&#8217;t easily generate reports on software usage to meet regulatory requirements.</li>
</ul>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-proactive-strategies-preventing-saas-sprawl-before-it-starts">Proactive strategies: Preventing SaaS sprawl before it starts</h2>



<p>The best way to manage SaaS sprawl is to prevent it in the first place. Here&#8217;s how to build a robust defense:</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-centralized-software-procurement-policy">Centralized software procurement policy</h3>



<p>Establish clear guidelines for acquiring new SaaS tools. Designate a central authority (often IT, Procurement, or a dedicated software asset management team) for all approvals. Implement a formal request and review process that requires vetting for security, compliance, integration needs, and existing redundancies.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-comprehensive-vendor-management">Comprehensive vendor management</h3>



<p>Don&#8217;t just buy and forget. Build strong relationships with key vendors. This allows you to negotiate bulk discounts and enterprise agreements, often saving significant money. Regularly review vendor performance and contracts to ensure you&#8217;re getting the most value.</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Employee education &amp; training</h3>



<p>Ignorance isn&#8217;t bliss when it comes to SaaS sprawl. Raise awareness about the risks of shadow IT, emphasizing the security and compliance implications. Promote your approved tools and their proper usage, and encourage employees to report new software needs through official channels, rather than signing up on their own.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Budgeting and forecasting</h3>



<p><a href="/monitor/the-ultimate-guide-to-saas-budgeting/">Get granular with your SaaS spending</a>. Allocate specific budgets for SaaS applications and track expenditures meticulously. Forecast future needs based on company growth and strategic initiatives, and implement cost-center tracking for all SaaS expenses to understand where your money is going.</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Reactive strategies: Taming existing SaaS sprawl</h2>



<p>So you’ve got existing sprawl. It’s time for some aggressive intervention.</p>



<p>To start, you can’t manage what you don’t know you have. Which is why a discovery phase is a crucial starting point. Manually reviewing expense reports, analyzing network traffic, and asking around to other employees is a hunt worth having, but can be a time suck. Automated tools, like <a href="/what-is-a-saas-management-platform/">SaaS management platforms (SMPs)</a>, can automatically discover applications in use across your network.</p>



<p>The goal is to create a comprehensive inventory of the tool’s name, the owner, its cost, purpose, usage analytics, and the renewal date. This foundational step provides the necessary visibility for your team to act on taming sprawl.</p>



<p>Once inventoried, understand how each tool is being used. Tracking not only underutilized applications, but active users versus licensed users for each application.</p>



<p>Leveraging this usage data is what helps make informed renewal decisions. Why pay for 100 licenses when only 20 are actively used?</p>



<p>With a comprehensive inventory and enriched usage data in your hands, you’re ready to make the tough call. <a href="/monitor/taming-the-it-beast-strategies-for-consolidating-and-simplifying-your-tech-stack/">Eliminate redundant or unused tools, consolidate similar applications</a>, and optimize licenses by reducing counts or switching to more cost-effective tiers.</p>



<p>Now you’ve got yourself a lean <s>and mean</s> tech stack, but to really prevent SaaS sprawl from happening again, there should be a responsible party who oversees every SaaS application’s usage, cost, and security. Defining clear roles and responsibilities for software management within your organization will be the only way to prevent sprawl from happening again.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Best practices for ongoing SaaS sprawl management</h2>



<p>Managing SaaS sprawl isn&#8217;t a one-time project; it&#8217;s an ongoing commitment.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Regular audits and reviews</h3>



<p>Schedule quarterly or semi-annual reviews of all your SaaS applications. Look for newly acquired tools, changing usage patterns, and potential security vulnerabilities. This proactive approach helps catch sprawl before it spirals out of control.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Foster a culture of collaboration</h3>



<p>Encourage open communication between departments and IT. Promote the sharing of best practices and successful tool implementations. When everyone understands the benefits of controlled SaaS usage, they&#8217;re more likely to participate.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Leverage automation</h3>



<p>Wherever possible, <a href="/monitor/the-value-of-no-code-in-it-automation/">automate tasks related to SaaS management</a>. This includes automating onboarding and offboarding for SaaS tools and automating license provisioning and de-provisioning based on employee status. This reduces manual effort and improves accuracy.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Stay informed</h3>



<p>The SaaS landscape is constantly evolving. Keep up with new SaaS trends, emerging security threats, and innovative solutions. Continuously evaluate new tools that could offer better value, enhanced functionality, or consolidate existing services.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Implement a SaaS management platform</h3>



<p>For larger organizations, an SMP can be a game-changer. These platforms offer:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>Centralized visibility</strong> into all your SaaS applications.</li>



<li><strong>Automated discovery</strong> of new tools.</li>



<li><strong>Cost optimization</strong> features, including usage tracking and renewal alerts.</li>



<li><strong>License management</strong> to ensure you&#8217;re not over- or under-licensed.</li>



<li><strong>Security insights</strong> to identify potential risks. When choosing an SMP, look for features that align with your specific needs.</li>
</ul>



<p>Understanding <a href="/monitor/how-smps-save-time-money/">how SMPs save time and money</a> can justify the investment in such platforms.</p>



<p>Successfully managing SaaS sprawl is crucial for controlling costs, bolstering security, and improving overall operational efficiency. It&#8217;s an ongoing process that requires vigilance, clear policies, and the right tools. By implementing these strategies, you can gain control of your SaaS ecosystem and ensure your software investments are truly working for you.</p>
