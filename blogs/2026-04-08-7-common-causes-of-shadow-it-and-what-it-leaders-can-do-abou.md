<p>Shadow IT, shmadow IT—we all know it’s happening.</p>



<p>Employees are adopting tools outside of IT’s visibility every day. Not because they’re trying to be difficult, but because they’re trying to get work done.</p>



<p>The real question isn’t <em>if</em> <a href="https://www.bettercloud.com/monitor/managing-shadow-it/">shadow IT</a> exists. It’s <em>why</em>. And more importantly, what’s causing it in the first place? Because once you understand the drivers, you can start addressing <a href="https://www.bettercloud.com/monitor/hidden-risks-shadow-it/">the problem at its root</a>, not just reacting to it after the fact.</p>



<p>Here are seven of the most common causes of shadow IT and what IT leaders can do about each one.</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-1-slow-procurement-and-approval-processes-create-workarounds">1. Slow procurement and approval processes create workarounds</h2>



<p>When employees encounter friction in getting the tools they need, they rarely just wait patiently. Work doesn’t stop, so they find another way.</p>



<p>Lengthy procurement cycles, unclear approval processes, and multiple layers of review can turn a simple request into a weeks-long ordeal. From the employee’s perspective, signing up for a tool themselves takes minutes. From IT’s perspective, there are valid concerns such as security reviews, compliance checks, and vendor vetting.</p>



<p>But when the gap between minutes and weeks gets too wide, shadow IT fills it.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-what-it-leaders-can-do">What IT leaders can do</h3>



<p>Speed and clarity matter just as much as control. Start by streamlining your intake and approval workflows. This could mean standardized request forms, pre-approved vendors, or tiered review processes based on risk level.</p>



<p>Many organizations are also adopting fast lanes for low-risk SaaS tools, where approvals can happen in hours instead of weeks. Pair that with clear SLAs so employees know what to expect.</p>



<p>Just as important is making the process easy and accessible for end users. Self-service options, like the <a href="https://www.bettercloud.com/monitor/it-automation-with-slack-part-three/">BetterCloud Self-Service Agent</a>, allow employees to kick off requests and trigger workflows directly without needing to navigate complex systems or wait on back-and-forth communication. This helps IT maintain governance while giving users a faster path to the tools they need.</p>



<p>The goal isn’t to eliminate governance. It is to remove unnecessary friction so employees do not feel like going around IT is their only option.</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-2-gaps-between-the-official-stack-and-real-workflows">2. Gaps between the official stack and real workflows</h2>



<p>Even the most thoughtfully curated tech stack cannot account for every edge case. Over time, teams evolve, workflows change, and new needs emerge that were not part of the original plan.</p>



<p>When the tools IT provides do not quite fit how work actually happens, employees start patching together their own solutions. Maybe it is a niche project management app, a specialized design tool, or a lightweight automation platform.</p>



<p>Individually, these decisions make sense. Collectively, they create fragmentation and shadow IT.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-what-it-leaders-can-do-0">What IT leaders can do</h3>



<p>This is less about enforcement and more about alignment. Regularly engage with business units to understand how work is actually getting done, not just how it is supposed to happen.</p>



<p>Conduct periodic workflow audits or tool usage reviews. Where are employees improvising? Where are they exporting data, duplicating work, or relying on manual processes?</p>



<p>From there, you have options:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>Expand your approved stack to better meet real needs</li>



<li>Consolidate overlapping tools</li>



<li>Formally adopt tools that are already proving valuable</li>
</ul>



<p>The key is to treat shadow IT as a signal, not just a problem. It often highlights gaps your official stack has not addressed yet.</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-3-lack-of-awareness-of-existing-tools-leads-to-redundancy">3. Lack of awareness of existing tools leads to redundancy</h2>



<p>Sometimes shadow IT is not about dissatisfaction. It is about visibility.</p>



<p>In many organizations, employees do not have a clear understanding of what tools are already available to them. They may not know that their company already has a license for a similar or better solution. So they go out and find one themselves.</p>



<p>The result is duplicate tools, wasted spend, and unnecessary complexity.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-what-it-leaders-can-do-1">What IT leaders can do</h3>



<p>If your approved stack is not visible and easy to navigate, it might as well not exist.</p>



<p>Create a centralized, accessible catalog of approved tools. This could be an internal portal, a self-service app marketplace, or integrations within tools employees already use such as Slack or Teams.</p>



<p>But visibility alone is not enough. You also need enablement:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>Provide onboarding resources and use-case examples</li>



<li>Highlight underutilized tools</li>



<li>Communicate regularly about what is available and why</li>
</ul>



<p>When employees know what they have and how to use it, they are far less likely to go searching elsewhere.</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-4-saas-is-too-easy-to-adopt">4. SaaS is too easy to adopt</h2>



<p>With great ease comes… a surprising amount of complexity.</p>



<p>Today, anyone can sign up for a tool in minutes. No infrastructure, no installation, and no approval required. Just an email address and a credit card.</p>



<p>This ease of adoption empowers teams, but it also bypasses traditional IT controls entirely. What used to require coordination with IT can now happen independently and at scale.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-what-it-leaders-can-do-2">What IT leaders can do</h3>



<p>You cannot and should not try to eliminate this reality. SaaS agility is a feature, not a bug. The goal is to build visibility and guardrails around it.</p>



<p>Start by implementing <a href="https://www.bettercloud.com/monitor/shadow-it-detection-guide/">SaaS discovery mechanisms</a> to understand what is actually being used across your environment. This might include analyzing SSO logs, expense data, or <a href="https://www.bettercloud.com/monitor/bettercloud-chrome-browser-extension/">browser traffic</a>.</p>



<p>Then, focus on governance:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>Define clear policies for tool adoption</li>



<li>Encourage employees to involve IT early</li>



<li>Provide guidance on what is acceptable versus high risk</li>
</ul>



<p>Instead of fighting SaaS accessibility, meet it with proportional oversight. Make it just as easy to do things the right way as it is to do them independently.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-5-decentralized-purchasing-masks-true-saas-spend">5. Decentralized purchasing masks true SaaS spend</h2>



<p>In many organizations, SaaS purchasing is no longer centralized. Individual departments or teams can expense tools directly, often without IT’s knowledge.</p>



<p>This decentralization makes it difficult to answer basic questions:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>What tools are we actually paying for?</li>



<li>How many licenses are being used?</li>



<li>Where are we overspending or duplicating functionality?</li>
</ul>



<p>Without visibility into spend, shadow IT becomes financially invisible even as costs continue to grow.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-what-it-leaders-can-do-3">What IT leaders can do</h3>



<p>Visibility into SaaS spend is foundational. Work closely with finance and procurement teams to aggregate data from expense reports, corporate cards, and vendor contracts.</p>



<p>From there, build a more centralized view of your SaaS portfolio, even if purchasing itself remains partially decentralized.</p>



<p>You can also introduce lightweight controls:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>Require IT review above certain spend thresholds</li>



<li>Establish preferred vendor lists</li>



<li>Offer incentives for teams to adopt approved tools</li>
</ul>



<p>The goal is not to strip autonomy from business units. It is to ensure that autonomy does not come at the expense of visibility and efficiency.</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-6-cultural-perception-of-it-drives-avoidable">6. Cultural perception of IT drives avoidable</h2>



<p><strong>Technology decisions are not just operational. They are cultural</strong>.</p>



<p>If IT is perceived as slow, overly restrictive, or disconnected from business needs, employees are more likely to work around it. Shadow IT in this case becomes less about tools and more about trust.</p>



<p>On the other hand, when IT is seen as a partner who enables productivity rather than blocking it, employees are far more likely to engage proactively.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-what-it-leaders-can-do-4">What IT leaders can do</h3>



<p><strong>This is one of the hardest but most impactful areas to address</strong>.</p>



<p>Start by examining how IT is experienced across the organization. Are requests met with “no” or with “let’s figure this out”? Are policies clearly explained, or do they feel arbitrary?</p>



<p>Practical steps include:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>Embedding IT liaisons within business units</li>



<li>Hosting regular feedback sessions</li>



<li>Communicating the reasoning behind decisions, not just the decisions themselves</li>
</ul>



<p>Small changes in tone and transparency can have an outsized impact. When employees feel heard and supported, shadow IT becomes less appealing.</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-7-remote-and-hybrid-work-accelerate-tool-sprawl">7. Remote and hybrid work accelerate tool sprawl</h2>



<p>The shift to remote and hybrid work has dramatically increased reliance on SaaS tools. Collaboration, communication, and project management now happen through software.</p>



<p>With distributed teams operating across time zones and functions, the demand for flexible and specialized tools has grown quickly. Without strong visibility, so has tool sprawl.</p>



<p>What might have once been a manageable number of applications can quickly grow into hundreds, many of them untracked.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-what-it-leaders-can-do-5">What IT leaders can do</h3>



<p>In a distributed environment, visibility and automation become critical.</p>



<p>Invest in systems that provide a unified view of your SaaS ecosystem, including who is using what, how often, and with what level of access.</p>



<p>From there, focus on lifecycle management:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><a href="https://www.bettercloud.com/monitor/getting-to-zero-touch/">Automate onboarding and offboarding processes</a></li>



<li><a href="https://www.bettercloud.com/software-license-management/">Regularly review and right-size licenses</a></li>



<li><a href="https://www.bettercloud.com/monitor/how-to-find-inactive-saas-accounts/">Deprovision unused or redundant tools</a></li>
</ul>



<p>Remote work is not going away. Managing tool sprawl in this context requires scalable and automated approaches, not manual oversight.</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-turn-insight-into-action">Turn insight into action</h2>



<p>Shadow IT is not just a technical problem. It is an organizational one. It emerges at the intersection of process, culture, and technology.</p>



<p>While it is easy to frame shadow IT as something to eliminate, the reality is more nuanced. In many cases, it reflects employees trying to be resourceful, efficient, and productive.</p>



<p>For IT leaders, the goal is not to shut that down. It is to channel it.</p>



<p>By addressing the root causes, reducing friction, improving visibility, aligning with real workflows, and building trust, you can shift from reactive enforcement to proactive enablement.</p>



<p>Because the organizations that manage shadow IT most effectively are not the ones with the strictest controls. They are the ones that make it easy for employees to do the right thing in the first place.</p>
