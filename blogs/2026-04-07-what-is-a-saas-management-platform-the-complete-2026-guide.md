<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-the-tl-dr-on-what-is-a-saas-management-platform">The TL;DR on what is a SaaS management platform</h2>



<p>A <a href="https://www.bettercloud.com/">SaaS management platform (SMP)</a> is a unified software solution for IT teams to centrally discover, manage, and secure all Software-as-a-Service (SaaS) applications across an organization.</p>



<p>In short, an SMP is a vital tool that helps organizations:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>Optimize spend:</strong> Easily identify and reclaim unused licenses, reduce shadow IT, and gain complete visibility into all SaaS expenditures.</li>



<li><strong>Boost IT efficiency:</strong> Automate routine, error-prone operational tasks like user onboarding/offboarding, saving time and allowing IT to scale.</li>



<li><strong>Reduce security risk: </strong>Automatically remediate threats and sensitive data oversharing across the entire SaaS landscape.&nbsp;</li>



<li><strong>Strengthen governance: </strong>Establish consistent rules and accountability for access, data handling, and configuration across all SaaS applications.</li>
</ul>



<p>With the average organization using over 100 SaaS apps, an SMP moves IT from reactive, manual processes and fragmented control to a proactive, automated, and secure management environment. What is a SaaS management platform? A&nbsp;<a href="https://www.bettercloud.com/what-is-a-saas-management-platform/">SaaS management platform</a>&nbsp;is a centralized administration software that provides a unified view of an organization’s SaaS (Software-as-a-Service) landscape, enabling IT teams to enforce security policies, manage user access, and optimize spending.</p>



<p>Many definitions of this established technology category have been floating around. But as nearly every company nowadays relies on SaaS, it’s getting clearer what a SaaS Management Platform (SMP) is and what it should do for SaaS Operations (<a href="https://www.bettercloud.com/what-is-saasops/">SaaSOps</a>).</p>



<p>Back in 2017,&nbsp;leading analyst firm Gartner&nbsp;first reported on a new category called “Cloud Office Management Tools”. Then the following year in 2018, they renamed this class of tools to be SaaS Management Platforms (SMPs).&nbsp;</p>



<p>Gartner continues to evolve its definition of this tool class. In 2025, <a href="https://www.gartner.com/en/documents/6790734">Gartner</a> now includes functionalities such as cost optimization, employee self-service via an application store, data-driven insights to improve user adoption and onboarding/offboarding tasks. Into 2026, SMPs increasingly address challenges like generative AI usage, governance, overspend, elevated risk, lack of visibility, contract sprawl, and more.</p>



<p>So now most agree that SaaS management software is a standalone tool that can discover, manage, and secure multiple SaaS applications from a central admin dashboard.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">With no SaaS management platform, what happens?</h2>



<p>IT teams fly blind. Organizations are encumbered with&nbsp;<a href="/resources/the-saas-explosion/">unauthorized SaaS and unknown risk</a>.</p>



<p>With no available SaaS usage insight, there’s no way to identify all apps, much less the redundant or unused SaaS apps that only cost them. Further, it’s nearly impossible to control SaaS spend or consistently manage SaaS apps.</p>



<p>So this brings us to the second problem: organizations without an SMP experience: IT can’t manage what they can’t see.</p>



<p>IT teams struggle to manage authorized apps—the ones they do know about—with a mishmash of manual processes within individual SaaS apps. With an&nbsp;<a href="/resources/state-of-saas/">average of 106&nbsp;SaaS apps</a>&nbsp;in use, this means IT teams are prisoner to individual app admin consoles and spreadsheets. All, of course, operate very differently, offering varying levels of sophistication around visibility and control, making it impossible to standardize IT processes like&nbsp;<a href="/monitor/anatomy-of-the-perfect-bettercloud-offboarding-workflow/">user offboarding</a>.&nbsp;</p>



<p>With no visibility into apps, files, groups, and&nbsp;<a href="/monitor/how-to-think-about-saasops-security/">user interactions</a>&nbsp;or management uniformity, there are new problems.&nbsp;</p>



<p>Without a SaaS management platform, human error creeps in, reducing IT efficiency while threatening an organization’s security posture because it’s impossible to guarantee data security. No IT team can possibly be agile enough to address threats as they arise. And ultimately both the organization’s productivity and security decline.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">What is a SaaS management platform, and what does it do?</h2>



<p>An SMP is essentially a unified governance and automation layer placed over your entire SaaS stack.&nbsp;</p>



<p>At its core, a SaaS management platform provides a central place to automatically:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>Discover SaaS apps in use throughout the organization</li>



<li>Manage users, apps, spend, files, and user interactions with company data</li>



<li>Secure company data</li>
</ul>



<p>In this section, we review use cases and benefits of using an all-in-one SaaS management platform.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-common-saas-management-use-cases-for-smps-nbsp">Common SaaS management use cases for SMPs&nbsp;</h3>



<p>You can rely on an SMP to perform many tasks like:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>Spend reporting:</strong> Improve budget allocation with auditable spend reporting across all applications including <a href="/use-case/saas-cost-control/">narrowing down spend by employee or department</a>.</li>



<li><strong>Spend and license optimization</strong>: Pair software&nbsp;<a href="/monitor/your-guide-to-make-software-spend-optimization-work-for-your-company/">usage insights with automated policies</a>&nbsp;to flag tool overlaps,&nbsp;<a href="/monitor/software-sanity-check-track-reclaim-renew/">eliminate unused licenses, and with it, the money spent</a></li>



<li><strong>Least privilege access enforcement:</strong>&nbsp;Limit administrator access to settings and controls needed for the individual to do their job. Auto-apply policies to limit admin rights, group memberships, and permissions; revoke unused access after set periods.</li>



<li><strong>SaaS governance and configuration management</strong><strong>:</strong>&nbsp;Ensure user, group, and file settings are correct from the start and remain secure.</li>



<li><strong>File sharing security remediation and data exposure alerts:</strong> Discover <a href="/monitor/the-hidden-costs-of-insecure-file-sharing-a-horror-story/">sensitive content stored across SaaS applications</a> and <a href="/platform/file-governance/">automatically remediate sensitive information oversharing</a>, as well as automatically detect and respond to threats to always comply with laws and standards.</li>



<li><strong>Compliance and audit support</strong> &#8211; Automating reporting, policy enforcement, and continuous monitoring to maintain standards like SOC 2, GDPR, or HIPAA.</li>



<li><strong>User lifecycle management (on/offboarding and mid-cycle changes):</strong>&nbsp;<a href="/use-case/onboarding-offboarding-automation/">Automate onboard, offboard, and mid-lifecycle user change processes</a>&nbsp;to reduce human error, risk, and save valuable time.</li>
</ul>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading">SMPs act as a governance layer in the IT stack</h3>



<p>As such, to accomplish these tasks, SaaS management platforms <a href="/monitor/where-does-bettercloud-fit-into-your-tech-stack/">integrate with several other systems</a>. <p>Additionally, a SaaS management platform must discover SaaS apps and integrate them for management. To accomplish this, it must be able to constantly ingest large quantities of data, normalize it, and graph all data objects across the SaaS environment.</p>



<p>This process is the foundation of operational context critical to effectively managing and securing applications.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">How can automating SaaS workflows improve security posture?</h2>



<p>Automating SaaS workflows directly strengthens security by minimizing human error, enforcing policies consistently, and enabling proactive responses.&nbsp;</p>




<img alt="BetterCloud content scanning for sensitive content such as profanity, credit cards, and more." class="imgBorderShadow" src="/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/content-scanning-screenshot.png" />



<p>The main ways it improves security posture:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>Reduces manual errors and delays</strong> — Automates offboarding to instantly revoke access (preventing insider threats or orphaned accounts) and enforces least-privilege rules without oversight gaps.</li>



<li><strong>Enforces consistent policies at scale</strong> — Applies uniform security configurations (e.g., file sharing restrictions, MFA requirements) across all apps, eliminating inconsistencies from manual admin work.</li>



<li><strong>Enables real-time monitoring and remediation</strong> — Triggers automated alerts and actions on suspicious behavior, oversharing, or compliance violations, containing threats faster than manual processes.</li>



<li><strong>Limits excessive permissions</strong> — Auto-detects/removes unnecessary admin rights or public shares, aligning with zero-trust principles.</li>



<li><strong>Supports continuous compliance</strong> — Automates audits, reporting, and remediation to maintain standards, reducing risk during rapid SaaS changes or employee turnover.</li>



<li><strong>Mitigates insider and external risks</strong> — Timely automation (e.g., deprovisioning on departure) prevents data leaks, while usage-based revocation curbs unauthorized access.</li>
</ul>



<p>In 2026&#8217;s threat landscape, workflow automation shifts security from reactive to preventive, significantly lowering overall risk.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-faqs-on-what-is-a-saas-management-platform">FAQs on what is a SaaS management platform</h2>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-q-what-is-a-saas-management-platform">Q: What is a SaaS Management Platform?</h3>



<p>A: SaaS Management Platforms are tools that help IT teams discover, manage, and secure SaaS applications across an organization.</p>



<p>SMPs provide:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>SaaS application discovery</li>



<li>User access visibility</li>



<li>Automated onboarding and offboarding</li>



<li>Privilege auditing</li>



<li>Policy enforcement across SaaS environments</li>
</ul>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-q-why-use-a-saas-management-platform">Q: Why use a SaaS management platform?</h3>



<p>A: Organizations use a SaaS management platform to centralize <strong>governance</strong> and control over their entire SaaS environment. Key reasons include:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>Optimizing SaaS spend:</strong> Gaining complete visibility into application usage to identify and reclaim unused licenses, reduce shadow IT, and lower overall software costs.</li>



<li><strong>Increasing IT efficiency:</strong> Automating critical and repetitive SaaSOps tasks, such as user lifecycle management (onboarding and offboarding), which reduces manual effort and human error.</li>



<li><strong>Reducing security risk:</strong> Enforcing consistent security policies, managing least-privilege access, detecting data oversharing, and automatically remediating threats across all connected applications.</li>



<li><strong>Enhancing visibility:</strong> Providing a single pane of glass for discovering, managing, and securing all sanctioned and unsanctioned SaaS applications, users, and data interactions.</li>
</ul>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-q-what-features-should-i-look-for-in-a-saas-management-platform">Q: What features should I look for in a SaaS management platform?</h3>



<p>A: The essential features you should prioritize in a SaaS management platform are those that ensure comprehensive visibility, strong governance, efficient automation, and robust security, especially given the rise of generative <strong>AI</strong> tools. Look for a platform with:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>No-code workflow automation:</strong> For streamlining routine tasks like onboarding/offboarding, license reclamation, and cross-application security actions.</li>



<li><strong>Comprehensive app discovery:</strong> Methods (like OAuth, expense integrations, browser extensions) to find and categorize all sanctioned and shadow IT.</li>



<li><strong>Centralized spend optimization:</strong> Tools for usage analytics, unused license reclamation, contract tracking, and AI-enhanced savings recommendations.</li>



<li><strong>Deep integrations:</strong> Extensive connectivity to Identity Providers, HRIS, ITSM, and core SaaS suites (Google Workspace, Microsoft 365) to ensure a unified view.</li>



<li><strong>Advanced security capabilities:</strong> Sensitive data scanning, real-time alerts, least-privilege enforcement, automated remediation, and compliance support.</li>



<li><strong>Scalability and Extensibility:</strong> An architecture that supports a growing SaaS portfolio, custom applications, and the governance of emerging <strong>AI</strong> agents and native-AI tools.</li>
</ul>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-q-what-are-the-most-common-use-cases-for-saas-management-automation">Q: What are the most common use cases for SaaS management automation?</h3>



<p>A: The most common use cases for SaaS management automation center on efficiency, security, and cost control across the SaaS lifecycle. They include:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>User lifecycle Management (ULM):</strong> Fully automating the onboarding, offboarding, and mid-cycle change processes across all connected SaaS applications to ensure immediate access revocation (critical for security) and consistent provisioning/deprovisioning.</li>



<li><strong>SaaS license reclamation and optimization:</strong> Automatically identifying dormant or underutilized user licenses based on usage data and taking action (e.g., deactivating or downgrading) to reclaim that spend and reduce overall software costs.</li>



<li><strong>Access governance and least-privilege enforcement:</strong> Consistently applying security policies by automatically adjusting user permissions, limiting administrative access, and revoking public file shares, which is vital for compliance and reducing risk, especially as <strong>AI</strong> tools introduce new vectors for data exposure.</li>



<li><strong>Shadow IT discovery and remediation:</strong> Continuously scanning and integrating with financial and network systems to detect unsanctioned applications and automatically enforcing policies to control their usage and associated risk.</li>
</ul>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-q-what-are-the-key-benefits-of-integrating-multiple-saas-applications-through-a-single-platform">Q: What are the key benefits of integrating multiple SaaS applications through a single platform?</h3>



<p>A: Integrating multiple SaaS applications through a single platform, like an SMP, delivers several key benefits focused on establishing unified <strong>governance</strong> and centralized <strong>control</strong> over the entire digital workplace. These benefits include:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>Centralized governance and control:</strong> Provides a &#8220;single pane of glass&#8221; view over all applications, users, files, and spend, allowing IT to enforce policies and manage the entire SaaS ecosystem from one place, thus eliminating fragmented control.</li>



<li><strong>Enhanced security and compliance:</strong> Ensures consistent security policy enforcement (e.g., least-privilege access, data sharing restrictions) across all integrated apps, significantly reducing the risk of data leaks and automatically supporting compliance with standards like GDPR or SOC 2.</li>



<li><strong>Improved operational efficiency:</strong> Streamlines complex, cross-application workflows (like onboarding/offboarding) through automation, eliminating manual, error-prone tasks and allowing IT to scale operations without proportional increase in complexity.</li>



<li><strong>Optimized spend and resource allocation:</strong> Offers comprehensive usage analytics across all applications, making it easy to identify redundant tools, reclaim unused licenses, and allocate budget based on actual needs, thereby maximizing ROI.</li>



<li><strong>Better data integrity and context:</strong> By normalizing and centralizing data from disparate apps, the platform provides deep operational context, which is essential for informed decision-making and precise, automated action (e.g., knowing <em>who</em> shared <em>what</em> file <em>where</em>).</li>
</ul>
